# DiceBound — Prologue Draft 0.1

> Exploratory prose for issue #94. Not locked player-facing copy.

---

Nobody knows where the Road begins.

This is mostly because everyone who went looking for the beginning eventually arrived somewhere else.

There are theories.

Kings built it.
Gods carved it.
Giants dragged it behind them.
A civilization older than language laid the stones according to a map of the stars.

One merchant claims it was commissioned badly and that somebody still owes him money.

You remember none of these stories when you wake beside the fire.

For several seconds you remember nothing at all.

Then the world returns in pieces.

Cold dirt beneath your back.
Smoke in your nose.
A terrible ache behind your eyes.
Something wet on your sleeve.

You raise your arm.

Blood.

Not much.

Enough.

You inspect yourself with the professional concern of someone who has no idea whether they are dying.

No obvious wound.

That is encouraging.

You decide not to investigate whose blood it is.

That decision feels practiced.

The fire crackles beside you.

It is a good fire.

This is the first truly suspicious thing.

There is no rain hissing on it. No wind pushing the flame flat. No half-burned log collapsing at the wrong moment. It simply burns with the quiet competence of something that has done the same job for a very long time and sees no reason to discuss it.

Around it sits a campsite.

A chest.
A rough table.
A bedroll.
Empty hooks where trophies might hang.
A few stones arranged with more care than necessary.

And, some distance away, a Road.

Not *a* road.

The Road.

You know this before you know your own name.

It stretches from the edge of the Camp between dark trees, bends around a hill, and disappears.

Behind you there is no Road at all.

There should be.

The Camp cannot simply exist at the end of nothing.

You walk twenty paces behind the fire to prove this.

The grass continues.

Thirty paces.

Rocks.

Forty.

A tree.

You circle the Camp.

The Road exists only in front of it.

This is the second suspicious thing.

You return to the fire.

Something hard presses into your palm.

You look down.

A die.

Six-sided.

Probably.

You count the faces.

One.
Two.
Three.
Four.
Five.
Six.

You turn it over and count again.

One.
Two.
Three.
Four.
Five.
Six.

This should not make you feel better.

It does.

The die is pale in your hand, though you cannot decide what it is made from. Bone, perhaps. Stone. Old ivory. Some dull metal warmed by your skin.

When you stop looking directly at it, the answer changes.

You put it down.

It lands on three.

The Road moves.

Not visibly.

That would be easier.

Instead, you feel the world make a decision.

A bird somewhere in the trees stops singing.

A stone that had been beside the Road is now on the other side.

The bend ahead is slightly sharper.

You stare.

Nothing moves again.

"No," you say.

Your voice sounds unfamiliar.

You pick up the die.

"Absolutely not."

The fire crackles.

It has no opinion.

A black-and-white cat walks out from beneath the table.

You had checked beneath the table.

You are almost certain of this.

The cat looks at you.

You look at the cat.

"Do you live here?"

The cat sits.

"Excellent. Good. Strong start."

It begins washing one paw.

You decide this is the closest thing available to local authority.

"Where am I?"

The cat continues washing.

"Do I live here?"

The cat pauses.

For one impossible moment, you are certain it looks offended by the question.

Then it walks to the chest, jumps on top, and curls up.

You open the chest.

It contains nothing.

The cat watches you discover this with the satisfaction of an employee whose warning email was ignored.

"Useful. Thank you."

You close the chest.

From the Road comes the sound of wheels.

You freeze.

The sound grows louder.

Wooden wheels.
A tired axle.
Something metallic clinking badly in a crate.

Around the bend comes a cart pulled by an animal you initially identify as a horse.

The longer you look at it, the less confident you become.

The driver wears a coat with too many pockets.

He sees you.

He sees the die in your hand.

His expression changes.

Not surprise.

Recognition.

Then, very quickly, irritation.

"Oh," he says.

The cart stops beside the Camp.

You wait.

He waits.

"Hello?" you offer.

The stranger sighs like a man discovering a recurring accounting error.

"You're early."

"For what?"

He ignores this and begins rummaging beneath the driver's seat.

"Do I know you?"

"Not currently."

You decide there are several parts of that sentence you dislike.

The stranger produces a small ledger, flips through pages covered in numbers, symbols and stains, then looks at you again.

"Any gold?"

"I don't know."

"Then no."

"Who are you?"

He seems surprised that this matters.

"Merchant."

"That's your name?"

"It's what you need."

"I need answers."

"Those are expensive."

"I have no gold."

"Then you don't need answers."

The cat opens one eye.

You point at the Road.

"Where does that go?"

The Merchant closes the ledger.

"Forward."

"And after that?"

"Still forward, if you're lucky."

"And if I'm not?"

He looks at the die.

"You'll find out."

The Merchant starts the cart moving again.

"Wait."

He does not.

"What is this?"

You hold up the die.

The cart stops.

The Merchant looks back.

For the first time, he is not amused.

"Don't lose it."

"What does it do?"

"Chooses."

"Chooses what?"

"Yes."

The cart rolls on.

You watch it disappear around the bend.

A few seconds later you hear the wheels stop.

Then you hear the Merchant shout from somewhere out of sight:

"AND IF YOU SEE THE DRAGON, TELL IT WE'RE EVEN!"

Silence.

You look at the cat.

The cat has gone back to sleep.

"Dragon."

Nothing.

"Of course there's a dragon."

The fire crackles.

The Road waits.

You consider staying at the Camp.

This is a reasonable plan.

There is water nearby, though you do not remember noticing it before. The chest is empty, but perhaps that can be improved. The cat appears to have survived here without becoming visibly dead.

There are worse lives.

Then you notice the marks on the table.

Scratches.

At first random.

Then rows.

Groups of six.

Dozens of them.

Hundreds.

Some old enough that the wood has grown dark around them.

You run a finger over the nearest group.

Five straight lines.

One slash across them.

Beneath it, carved much deeper than the rest:

**AGAIN.**

Your hand begins to shake.

You do not know why.

A memory surfaces.

Not an image.

Pain.

Teeth.

A wolf's eyes.

Then another memory.

A sword you have never held.

Then a third.

A sky filled with stars that do not exist above the Camp.

You stumble backward.

The die slips from your hand.

It hits the dirt.

Rolls once.

Twice.

Stops.

Six.

The Road moves.

This time you see it.

The trees ahead blur like reflections in disturbed water.

The bend straightens.

A ruined sign appears where there had been nothing.

Far down the Road, something howls.

The sound reaches the Camp a moment before the creature making it possibly could have heard you.

The cat wakes.

Its fur rises.

You stare at the six.

A thought arrives in your mind with the certainty of remembered fact.

Not words exactly.

A rule.

**Move.**

You pick up the die.

The Road waits for you to accept what it has already decided.

You look once at the Camp.

At the fire.
The empty chest.
The scratched table.
The cat.

"I'll be back," you say.

The words feel strange.

The cat looks away.

You step onto the Road.

Somewhere impossibly far ahead, something ancient opens its eyes.

And says:

"Again?"
